⚠ SCREENSHOTS CAN BE FAKED

A payment screenshot is not a payment.

Before you hand over goods or unlock a service, run the details past this screening tool — then confirm the only way that actually counts: your own bank or UPI app.

How the scam usually works

None of these require any technical skill — that's why the scam is so common at shop counters, on marketplace sites, and in delivery handoffs.

01

Edited screenshot

A real UPI app's "success" screen is copied and the amount, name, or time is changed before it's sent to you.

02

Look-alike app

A generic app is styled to resemble a bank or UPI app, generating a fake confirmation screen that was never sent by any bank.

03

Collect request, not payment

You're sent a "collect" request instead of receiving money — approving it on your end actually sends money out of your account.

04

Cancelled after the fact

A real payment is sent, you see it land, then it's reversed or disputed once the goods are already gone.

RESULT

    The only check that actually proves it

    A website, a screenshot, or an SMS forward can all be faked. These three things cannot.

    1. Open your own banking or UPI app directly

      Don't tap a link or open an app from the sender's message. Open the app icon on your own phone, from your own home screen.

    2. Check your balance or statement, not a message

      Look for the actual credit entry with a matching amount and time. A notification banner can be spoofed; a balance change on your own statement can't.

    3. If in doubt, wait it out

      Bank credits typically reflect within seconds to a couple of minutes. It's reasonable to ask a buyer to wait while you check — genuine payers won't mind.
